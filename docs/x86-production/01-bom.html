Bill of Materials Lenovo M910q Tiny — Alfa Romeo 156 Production Build

Complete parts list for the BCM v8.5 headunit based on a used Lenovo ThinkCentre M910q Tiny (i5-6400T, 8 GB DDR4, 256 GB NVMe). Prices in PLN, Q1 2026 estimates from Polish retailers and Allegro/AliExpress.

Why M910q?

A used M910q costs 200-400 PLN and offers a quad-core Intel i5 with VAAPI hardware decode, 2 DisplayPort outputs, 6 USB 3.0 ports, NVMe storage and reliable ACPI S3 suspend. The trade-off: no GPIO (requires Arduino peripherals) and a 20 V / 65 W supply, which in the car comes from a 12 V → 20 V step-up converter fed by the buffer bank.

Power items follow the buffered design

Rows P1–P10 are the buffered power system. The authoritative list, with every fuse and wire, is docs/ZASILANIE_BUFOROWANE.md (ZB) §10 (Polish); prices here are copied from it. Stage 5 Always-On Subsystem (window remote + BLE trunk)

Estimated cost: ~115 — 195 PLN

Replaces the previous "central lock + alarm" stage. The original Alfa 156 central lock and alarm are left untouched; this stage adds an always-on Arduino Nano (Domain A) powered from the buffered bus (5 V from the MP1584 buck) that handles the secondary window keyfob and the BLE-gated trunk-release button. Both work while the car is parked and the M910q (BCM) is asleep.

Platform Choice

Historyczne (nieaktualne) — the earlier M910q vs Orange Pi 5 Pro comparison

Kept only to explain why the project moved to the M910q. The Orange Pi build is retired and is not maintained; the prices and the “total BOM” row below are from that time and do not match the current stages above.

Aspect Lenovo M910q (x86) Orange Pi 5 Pro (ARM)
Cost (board only) 200-400 PLN (used) 350-450 PLN (new)
CPU Intel i5-6400T (4C/4T, 2.8 GHz boost) RK3588S (8-core ARM, big.LITTLE)
RAM / Storage 8 GB DDR4 / 256 GB NVMe 4 GB LPDDR4x / microSD or NVMe
GPU decode Intel HD 530 (VAAPI H.264/H.265) Mali G610 (V4L2 decode)
H.264 encode (dashcam) Software only (x264, CPU-bound) Hardware H.264 encode (MPP)
Display outputs 2× DisplayPort (need DP-to-HDMI adapters) 2× HDMI native (2.1 + 2.0)
GPIO None — requires Arduino peripherals 40-pin header, SPI, I2C, UART
USB ports 6× USB 3.0 + 1× USB-C 2× USB 3.0 + 1× USB 2.0
Power 20 V / 65 W (needs 12 V → 20 V boost) 5 V / 15 W (simple buck from 12 V)
WiFi / BT M.2 slot — swap Intel 8265 for Fenvi FU-AX1800 Built-in WiFi 6 + BT 5.0
Form factor 179×36.5×183 mm (1 litre) 100×75×20 mm (tiny)
Software compatibility Full x86 Debian, all packages ARM Debian, some packages need building
Total BOM (all stages) ~1 600 – 2 700 PLN ~1 950 – 3 165 PLN

Key trade-offs (at the time): The M910q is cheaper and more powerful for general compute, but lacks GPIO and requires a 12 V → 20 V boost converter. It also cannot do hardware H.264 encoding for the dashcam — the CPU handles encoding in software (x264), which works fine on an i5 but uses more power.

The OPi 5 Pro is smaller, runs on 5 V, has native GPIO, and has hardware H.264 encode, but costs more and has fewer USB ports.
