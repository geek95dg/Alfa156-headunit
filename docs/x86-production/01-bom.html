Bill of Materials Lenovo M910q Tiny — Alfa Romeo 156 Production Build

Complete parts list for the BCM v8.5 headunit based on a used Lenovo ThinkCentre M910q Tiny (i5-6400T, 8 GB DDR4, 256 GB NVMe). Prices in PLN, Q1 2026 estimates from Polish retailers and Allegro/AliExpress.

Why M910q?

A used M910q costs 200-400 PLN — cheaper than a new Orange Pi 5 Pro while offering a quad-core Intel i5 with VAAPI hardware decode, 2 DisplayPort outputs, 6 USB 3.0 ports, and NVMe storage. The trade-off: no GPIO (requires Arduino peripherals) and higher power draw (20V / 65W).

Stage 5 Always-On Subsystem (window remote + BLE trunk)

Estimated cost: ~100 — 160 PLN

Replaces the previous "central lock + alarm" stage. The original Alfa 156 central lock and alarm are left untouched; this stage adds an always-on Arduino Nano (Domain A) powered from the SLA battery buffer that handles the secondary window keyfob and the BLE-gated trunk-release button. Both work while the car is parked and the M910q (BCM) is asleep.

Key trade-offs

The M910q is cheaper and more powerful for general compute, but lacks GPIO and requires a 12 V → 20 V boost converter. It also cannot do hardware H.264 encoding for the dashcam — the CPU handles encoding in software (x264), which works fine on an i5 but uses more power.

The OPi 5 Pro is smaller, runs on 5 V, has native GPIO, and has hardware H.264 encode, but costs more and has fewer USB ports.
