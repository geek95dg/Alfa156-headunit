Physical Assembly Lenovo M910q Tiny — Mounting, Power, Cabling

Step-by-step guide to physically installing and wiring the M910q-based BCM headunit in an Alfa Romeo 156. Covers dash mounting, power supply chain, USB hub layout, display cabling, audio wiring, and WiFi module installation.

Safety first

Disconnect the car's negative battery terminal before doing any wiring. Use properly rated fuses on all power lines, each at the source end of its wire. The M910q draws up to 65 W at 20 V (6.6 A from the bank at 11 V) — the 200 W step-up and the 4 mm² wiring in section 2 are sized for that. The AGM bank can deliver ~2.2 kA into a short: cover its terminal blocks.

1.2 Mount Location Options in Alfa 156

Location Pros Cons Recommended
Behind centre console Short cable runs to displays; hidden from view; accessible from cabin Limited airflow; tight fit with HVAC ducting Yes (primary)
Under passenger seat Good airflow; easy access; plenty of space Longer cable runs; visible; vulnerable to spills/feet Fallback
Trunk Maximum space; easy access; cool environment Very long cable runs (USB signal loss); latency risk Not recommended

The buffer bank, chargers, LVD and the 20 V step-up live in the trunk, not next to the PC. The wire sizes in section 2 assume about 4 m from the trunk to the M910q (passenger footwell or behind the console); measure your route and re-check the voltage drop if it is longer.

1.3 Ventilation Requirements

The Intel i5-6400T has a 35 W TDP. The M910q's internal fan exhausts through the rear vent grille. Ensure:

  • Rear exhaust: Leave at least 30 mm clearance behind the unit for hot air to escape.
  • Intake: The M910q draws air from the bottom/sides. Do not place it on foam or carpet.
  • Ambient temperature: Summer cabin temperatures can exceed 60 °C when parked. The S3 suspend mode should be configured to kick in when ignition is off — the CPU does not generate heat in S3.
  • Mounting orientation: Horizontal (logo up) is preferred. Vertical mounting works but may slightly reduce fan efficiency.
Mounting tips

Use industrial Velcro strips (3M Dual Lock) or a custom 3D-printed bracket with M3 standoffs. The M910q has VESA 100 mount holes on its underside. Secure all cables with zip ties to prevent rattling over rough roads.

2. Power Wiring

Authoritative source

The full buffered power design — every fuse, wire size and test step — is docs/ZASILANIE_BUFOROWANE.md (ZB, Polish); terminal-by-terminal wiring is docs/SCHEMATY_POLACZEN.md. This page is a summary; where it differs, ZB wins.

2.1 Power Supply Chain

The M910q requires 20 V DC on its Lenovo slim-tip plug and works only in the 19–21 V window — it will not boot outside it. A potted 12 V → 20 V, 10 A step-up converter in the trunk feeds it from a bank of seven CSB HR1221W AGM batteries (35.7 Ah) in two steel baskets (4 + 3) bolted to the bulkhead behind the rear seat. Everything hangs on one buffered bus behind the LVD: there is no ignition relay in front of the loads. Ignition is only a signal — Nano #2 (sensor hub) turns it into a power-button pulse for S3 suspend and wake. See 10 — Power / Suspend.

Step-up converter check (there is nothing to adjust)

Potted 20 V modules usually have a fixed output and no potentiometer. Before connecting the M910q, measure each unit on the bench (ZB §3.4): no-load output must not exceed 20.5 V — reject the unit if it does, 21 V is the top of the M910q window. Load it to ~60 W and check its temperature, then lower the input to 11 V and see whether it still regulates. Over ~4 m of 4 mm² about 0.1 V is lost, so ~19.9 V reaches the plug; a 19 V module would end up below 19 V — do not buy one.

3. USB Hub Layout

The M910q has 6× USB 3.0 ports and 1× USB-C. A powered 7-port USB hub concentrates all peripherals that do not require dedicated bandwidth. 

M910q USB ports
  ├── USB Hub (powered, 7-port)
  │     ├── Arduino Pro Micro (input: SWC, buttons, encoder)
  │     ├── Arduino Nano #1 (output: window/trunk relays, backlight PWM)
  │     ├── Arduino Nano #2 (sensor hub: doors, ignition, rain, DS18B20,
  │     │                    power-button relay)
  │     ├── CP2102 (K-Line / OBD)
  │     ├── USB GPS (NEO-M8N)
  │     ├── USB LTE modem (Huawei E3372)
  │     └── USB microphone
  ├── USB DAC (ES9038Q2M) → RCA → ready-made car amplifier
  └── USB 4-ch AHD grabber (cameras)

3.1 Port Assignment Rationale

  • USB DAC — direct port: Connects directly to an M910q USB 3.0 port (not through the hub) to avoid audio latency and USB bandwidth contention. The ES9038Q2M is a USB Audio Class 2 device.
  • 4-ch AHD grabber — direct port: Also connects directly. Four simultaneous 720p video streams need sustained USB bandwidth. Sharing a hub would cause frame drops.
  • Hub peripherals: Low-bandwidth devices (serial Arduinos, GPS NMEA at 9600 baud, LTE via RNDIS, microphone at 16 kHz mono) share the powered hub without issues.
USB stability

Use udev rules to assign stable device names by USB path (not by kernel enumeration order). The BCM setup script creates rules in /etc/udev/rules.d/99-bcm-usb.rules. This ensures /dev/bcm-dac, /dev/bcm-gps, etc. survive reboots and re-plugging.

4. Display Cabling

The M910q has 2× DisplayPort outputs (mini-DP on some revisions, full-size DP on others; some units have HDMI — check yours). If the panels take HDMI and the PC has DP, passive DP-to-HDMI adapter cables are required. Resolutions come from display.dashboard and display.small in config/bcm_config.yaml.

4.1 Connections

M910q Output Adapter Display Signal
DP port 1 DP-to-HDMI passive cable 10.1" IPS touchscreen (1280×800) HDMI video + USB touch (separate USB cable)
DP port 2 DP-to-HDMI passive cable 6.86" widescreen (1280×480), optional, hot-plug HDMI video only (no touch)

4.2 Connector Naming

After the DP-to-HDMI adapters, the OS sees HDMI outputs. Connector names differ between tools:

Tool Main Display (10.1") Second Display (6.86")
xrandr HDMI-1 HDMI-2
DRM / KMS HDMI-A-1 HDMI-A-2
Verify connector names

After the OS is installed and both displays are connected, run: 

for f in /sys/class/drm/card*-*/status; do echo "$f: $(cat $f)"; done

This shows which DRM connectors are active. Update the BCM config (/opt/bcm/config/displays.conf) to match your actual connector names.

4.3 Touchscreen USB

The 10.1" touchscreen requires a separate USB cable for its touch digitizer. This connects to the M910q via the powered USB hub. The touch input is an HID device and appears as /dev/input/eventN. Map it to the correct display output using: 

xinput map-to-output "WaveShare WaveShare" HDMI-1

5.2 Wiring Details

  • USB DAC → RCA: The ES9038Q2M outputs line-level stereo via RCA jacks (red/white). Use shielded RCA cables to avoid alternator whine.
  • Amplifier input: RCA stereo in. The amplifier has 4 channels — front pair driven directly from left/right, rear pair from a Y-splitter (same signal) or via a balance control.
  • Amplifier outputs: 4 pairs of speaker wires to the car's existing speaker harness. Use the Alfa 156 ISO connector pinout:
Channel Amplifier Output Speaker Location Wire Colour (Alfa 156)
CH1 OUT1+/OUT1− Front Left White / White-Black
CH2 OUT2+/OUT2− Front Right Grey / Grey-Black
CH3 OUT3+/OUT3− Rear Left Green / Green-Black
CH4 OUT4+/OUT4− Rear Right Violet / Violet-Black

5.3 Subwoofer (Optional — Stage 4)

  • A ready-made mono car amplifier takes the same RCA signal as the main amplifier (Y-splitter).
  • Use the amplifier's own low-pass crossover if it has one (check your unit); without a subwoofer, set a ~80 Hz high-pass on all main channels instead.
  • Power it like the main amplifier: its own fused branch from the starter battery, never from the buffer bank.
  • Output drives a single 8 Ω or 4 Ω subwoofer driver.
Grounding — one star point, amplifier separate

The head unit has one ground point, in the trunk next to the bank (ZB §8.3): bank, chargers, XH-M603, LVD, distribution, step-up, the 20 V return from the M910q and the common 12 V return from the front all meet there, and one 6 mm² lead goes to bare body metal (not the floor above the fuel tank). The M910q, panels and front modules get no ground of their own to the body.

The amplifier is the exception: it has its own local ground, close to the amplifier, and is linked to the head unit only by the REM wire and the RCA screen. Run the RCA cable on the opposite side of the car from the amplifier power cable. If you hear whine that follows engine speed: fix the amplifier ground first, re-route the RCA second, and only then try a ground-loop isolator (it costs bandwidth).

6. Fenvi FU-AX1800 Installation

The M910q ships with an Intel 8265 WiFi/BT card in its M.2 Key E slot. For reliable 5 GHz AP mode (required by wireless Android Auto), replace it with the Fenvi FU-AX1800 (MediaTek MT7921, WiFi 6 + BT 5.2).

6.1 Swap Procedure

  1. Power off the M910q and unplug all cables.
  2. Remove the top cover (slide latch at rear, lift off).
  3. Locate the M.2 Key E slot — it is near the front-left of the motherboard, under the WiFi antenna cables.
  4. Disconnect the two IPEX/U.FL antenna pigtails from the Intel 8265 card. Use a plastic spudger or fingernails — do not use metal tools on the connectors.
  5. Remove the single M2×3 screw holding the Intel 8265 card.
  6. Slide the Intel 8265 out at a 30° angle.
  7. Insert the Fenvi FU-AX1800 into the M.2 Key E slot at the same 30° angle, then press flat and secure with the screw.
  8. Connect both IPEX/U.FL antenna pigtails to the Fenvi card. Press until they click.
  9. Replace the top cover.

6.2 Antenna Placement

  • The M910q's built-in antennas are wired to the rear panel. These work fine for WiFi client mode but may be suboptimal for AP mode with a phone in the cabin.
  • For better reception: route external antenna pigtails (IPEX to RP-SMA) out of the M910q case and place the antennas near the windshield (best line-of-sight to phone).
  • Use two antennas (2×2 MIMO) — the Fenvi FU-AX1800 has two IPEX connectors.
  • Keep antennas at least 20 cm apart for spatial diversity.

6.3 Driver Support

MediaTek MT7921 mt7921e (in-tree) 5.12+ Yes (5 GHz, non-DFS) BT 5.2 via btusb

# Expected: MediaTek Inc. MT7921 802.11ax PCI Express Wireless Network Adapter
