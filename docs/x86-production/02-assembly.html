Physical Assembly Lenovo M910q Tiny — Mounting, Power, Cabling

Step-by-step guide to physically installing and wiring the M910q-based BCM headunit in an Alfa Romeo 156. Covers dash mounting, power supply chain, USB hub layout, display cabling, audio wiring, and WiFi module installation.

Safety first

Disconnect the car's negative battery terminal before doing any wiring. Use properly rated fuses on all power lines. The M910q draws up to 65 W at 20 V — ensure your DC-DC converter and wiring can handle the current.

1.3 Ventilation Requirements

The Intel i5-6400T has a 35 W TDP. The M910q's internal fan exhausts through the rear vent grille. Ensure:

  • Rear exhaust: Leave at least 30 mm clearance behind the unit for hot air to escape.
  • Intake: The M910q draws air from the bottom/sides. Do not place it on foam or carpet.
  • Ambient temperature: Summer cabin temperatures can exceed 60 °C when parked. The S3 suspend mode should be configured to kick in when ignition is off — the CPU does not generate heat in S3.
  • Mounting orientation: Horizontal (logo up) is preferred. Vertical mounting works but may slightly reduce fan efficiency.
Mounting tips

Use industrial Velcro strips (3M Dual Lock) or a custom 3D-printed bracket with M3 standoffs. The M910q has VESA 100 mount holes on its underside. Secure all cables with zip ties to prevent rattling over rough roads.

2. Power Wiring

2.1 Power Supply Chain

The M910q requires 20 V DC via a barrel jack (not the standard 12 V car supply). An XL6019 boost converter steps up the 12 V rail. An AGM battery buffer provides cranking protection and S3 standby power. 

Car 12V ─[25A fuse]─┬─[Schottky diode MBR2045]─ AGM 12V 7.2Ah
                     │                              │
                     └───────────┬──────────────────┘
                                 │
                    ┌────────────┴────────────┐
                    │ LVD cutoff (10.5V)      │
                    └────────────┬────────────┘
                                 │
                   ┌─────────────┴────────────┐
                   │                           │
             [12V→20V boost]             [20A fuse]
                   │                           │
             M910q barrel jack           TDA7388 amp

2.2 Component Details

Component Purpose Rating
25 A blade fuse Main feed protection from car battery 25 A / 32 V
Schottky diode MBR2045 Prevents AGM backfeed to car battery during cranking 20 A / 45 V, 0.5 V drop
AGM 12 V 7.2 Ah Cranking buffer + S3 standby (~36 h) 12 V sealed lead-acid
LVD cutoff (10.5 V) Disconnects load when AGM drops below 10.5 V to prevent deep discharge 10.5 V threshold, 20 A

20 A blade fuse TDA7388 amplifier protection 20 A / 32 V
Boost converter tuning

Before connecting the M910q, adjust the XL6019 output to exactly 20.0 V using a multimeter. The M910q tolerates 19-21 V but will not boot outside that range. Secure the adjustment potentiometer with a drop of nail polish or thread locker to prevent vibration drift.

3. USB Hub Layout

The M910q has 6× USB 3.0 ports and 1× USB-C. A powered 7-port USB hub concentrates all peripherals that do not require dedicated bandwidth. 

M910q USB ports
  ├── USB Hub (powered, 7-port)
  │     ├── Arduino Pro Micro #1 (input: SWC, buttons, doors, sensors)
  │     ├── Arduino Nano #2 (output: relays, lock, lights, wipers)
  │     ├── USB GPS (NEO-M8N)
  │     ├── USB LTE modem (Huawei E3372)
  │     └── USB microphone
  ├── USB DAC (ES9038Q2M) → RCA → TDA7388 + TDA2050
  └── USB 4-ch AHD grabber (cameras)

3.1 Port Assignment Rationale

  • USB DAC — direct port: Connects directly to an M910q USB 3.0 port (not through the hub) to avoid audio latency and USB bandwidth contention. The ES9038Q2M is a USB Audio Class 2 device.
  • 4-ch AHD grabber — direct port: Also connects directly. Four simultaneous 720p video streams need sustained USB bandwidth. Sharing a hub would cause frame drops.
  • Hub peripherals: Low-bandwidth devices (serial Arduinos, GPS NMEA at 9600 baud, LTE via RNDIS, microphone at 16 kHz mono) share the powered hub without issues.
USB stability

Use udev rules to assign stable device names by USB path (not by kernel enumeration order). The BCM setup script creates rules in /etc/udev/rules.d/99-bcm-usb.rules. This ensures /dev/bcm-dac, /dev/bcm-gps, etc. survive reboots and re-plugging.

4. Display Cabling

The M910q has 2× DisplayPort outputs (mini-DP on some revisions, full-size DP on others). Both car displays use HDMI input, so passive DP-to-HDMI adapter cables are required.

4.1 Connections

M910q Output Adapter Display Signal
DP port 1 DP-to-HDMI passive cable 7" IPS touchscreen (1024×600) HDMI video + USB touch (separate USB cable)
DP port 2 DP-to-HDMI passive cable 4.3" TFT status display (800×480) HDMI video only (no touch)

4.2 Connector Naming

After the DP-to-HDMI adapters, the OS sees HDMI outputs. Connector names differ between tools:

Tool Main Display (7") Status Display (4.3")
xrandr HDMI-1 HDMI-2
DRM / KMS HDMI-A-1 HDMI-A-2
Verify connector names

After the OS is installed and both displays are connected, run: 

for f in /sys/class/drm/card*-*/status; do echo "$f: $(cat $f)"; done

This shows which DRM connectors are active. Update the BCM config (/opt/bcm/config/displays.conf) to match your actual connector names.

4.3 Touchscreen USB

The 7" touchscreen requires a separate USB cable for its touch digitizer. This connects to the M910q via the powered USB hub. The touch input is an HID device and appears as /dev/input/eventN. Map it to the correct display output using: 

xinput map-to-output "WaveShare WaveShare" HDMI-1

5. Audio Wiring

5.1 Signal Chain

M910q USB 3.0 port
       │
  ES9038Q2M USB DAC
       │
   RCA stereo out
       │
  ┌────┴─────────────────────────┐
  │                               │
  │  TDA7388 (4×45W)              │  TDA2050 (40W mono)
  │  Class AB amplifier           │  Subwoofer amplifier
  │                               │  (optional, Stage 4)
  ├──── FL speaker                │
  ├──── FR speaker                └──── Subwoofer
  ├──── RL speaker
  └──── RR speaker

5.2 Wiring Details

  • USB DAC → RCA: The ES9038Q2M outputs line-level stereo via RCA jacks (red/white). Use shielded RCA cables to avoid alternator whine.
  • TDA7388 input: RCA stereo in. The TDA7388 has 4 channels — front pair driven directly from left/right, rear pair wired in parallel (same signal) or via a balance control.
  • TDA7388 outputs: 4 pairs of speaker wires to the car's existing speaker harness. Use the Alfa 156 ISO connector pinout:
Channel TDA7388 Output Speaker Location Wire Colour (Alfa 156)
CH1 OUT1+/OUT1− Front Left White / White-Black
CH2 OUT2+/OUT2− Front Right Grey / Grey-Black
CH3 OUT3+/OUT3− Rear Left Green / Green-Black
CH4 OUT4+/OUT4− Rear Right Violet / Violet-Black

5.3 Subwoofer (Optional — Stage 4)

  • The TDA2050 takes the same RCA input as the TDA7388 (Y-splitter).
  • A passive low-pass filter (capacitor + resistor) at the TDA2050 input cuts frequencies above ~120 Hz.
  • Output drives a single 8 Ω or 4 Ω subwoofer driver.
Ground loops

To avoid alternator whine in the audio, ensure the USB DAC, amplifiers, and M910q share a single ground point on the car chassis. Do not use separate ground connections. Use a ground loop isolator on the RCA cable if whine persists.

6. Fenvi FU-AX1800 Installation

The M910q ships with an Intel 8265 WiFi/BT card in its M.2 Key E slot. For reliable 5 GHz AP mode (required by wireless Android Auto), replace it with the Fenvi FU-AX1800 (MediaTek MT7921, WiFi 6 + BT 5.2).

6.1 Swap Procedure

  1. Power off the M910q and unplug all cables.
  2. Remove the top cover (slide latch at rear, lift off).
  3. Locate the M.2 Key E slot — it is near the front-left of the motherboard, under the WiFi antenna cables.
  4. Disconnect the two IPEX/U.FL antenna pigtails from the Intel 8265 card. Use a plastic spudger or fingernails — do not use metal tools on the connectors.
  5. Remove the single M2×3 screw holding the Intel 8265 card.
  6. Slide the Intel 8265 out at a 30° angle.
  7. Insert the Fenvi FU-AX1800 into the M.2 Key E slot at the same 30° angle, then press flat and secure with the screw.
  8. Connect both IPEX/U.FL antenna pigtails to the Fenvi card. Press until they click.
  9. Replace the top cover.

6.2 Antenna Placement

  • The M910q's built-in antennas are wired to the rear panel. These work fine for WiFi client mode but may be suboptimal for AP mode with a phone in the cabin.
  • For better reception: route external antenna pigtails (IPEX to RP-SMA) out of the M910q case and place the antennas near the windshield (best line-of-sight to phone).
  • Use two antennas (2×2 MIMO) — the Fenvi FU-AX1800 has two IPEX connectors.
  • Keep antennas at least 20 cm apart for spatial diversity.
