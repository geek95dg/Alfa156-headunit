Power & Suspend Power supply, battery buffer, S3 suspend/resume, and ignition watcher for M910q in-car deployment

Overview

The Lenovo M910q uses a 65 W, 20 V barrel jack power supply — not a standard ATX connector. In the car, this means you need a 12 V → 20 V boost converter to step up the vehicle's 12 V supply to the required 20 V input.

Because the car's 12 V rail drops to 6–8 V during engine cranking, a 12 V SLA battery buffer sits between the car battery and the rest of the system. The buffer is built from 4–6 sealed lead-acid 12 V 5 Ah batteries wired in parallel (total 20–30 Ah; 5 packs = 25 Ah is the sweet spot). A Schottky diode isolates the bus from the car battery so it never backfeeds. A low-voltage disconnect (LVD) at 11.0 V protects the SLA bank from deep discharge when the car is parked for extended periods.

The bus splits into two power domains:

  • Domain A — always on. The Arduino Nano, HM-10 BLE module, RXB6 433 MHz receiver, 9 relays (4 windows × 2 directions + trunk), and the trunk-button input. Idle draw ~60 mA. This subsystem continues running while the BCM is asleep so the secondary window keyfob and BLE-gated trunk button keep working.
  • Domain B — ignition/RTC-controlled. The M910q, Pro Micro, USB hub, displays, amplifier. Comes up on ACC (via the ignition relay) or on M910q RTC wake. Draws ~10 W when up, 0 W when off.

The M910q supports native ACPI S3 suspend with a typical draw of only ~0.2 A at 12 V, but in this build we don't rely on it for parked-car standby — instead the ignition relay fully de-energises Domain B on park, and the BCM cold-boots on ACC or RTC wake. Domain A keeps the always-on features alive without involving the M910q at all.

Parked-car standby budget

At 60 mA Domain A draw, a 25 Ah pack (5 batteries) lasts ~17 days before reaching 50 % depth-of-discharge. Discharge past 50 % shortens SLA cycle life sharply, so the 11.0 V LVD trips well before the bus is empty. 4 batteries (20 Ah) gives ~14 days; 6 batteries (30 Ah) gives ~21 days. If you also let the M910q RTC-wake every 15 minutes for cloud tracking, halve those figures.

Key Design Points

  • Schottky diode (MBR2045): Prevents the buffer bus from backfeeding into the car battery. The diode's low forward voltage drop (~0.45 V) minimises power loss. One-way isolator only — it does not limit charge current, so the CC-CV stage below is mandatory.
  • CC-CV charge controller (mandatory): the Alfa 156 alternator is rated for 120 A. With the SLA bus depleted and the engine started, the Schottky alone would let the alternator dump tens of amps per pack until bus voltage rises — instant Schottky failure and cooked SLAs (cycle life collapses past 1 C charge). A DC-DC CC-CV buck module sits between the diode and the bus, limiting bulk-charge to ~15-20 A and stepping through 14.4 V absorb → 13.8 V float per SLA datasheet. Without this stage expect ~6-12 month SLA life instead of multi-year. An XL4016-based module (~30-50 PLN) is the DIY option; a proper B2B charger (Redarc BCDC, Sterling, Victron Orion-Tr Smart, ~600-1500 PLN) is bullet-proof and temperature-compensated.
  • LVD cutoff (11.0 V): Protects the SLA bank from deep discharge. SLA cycle life collapses past 50 % DoD; 11.0 V trips well before that point. The cutoff isolates all loads (both domains) so the bank stays parked at ~50 % SoC until the alternator can recharge.
  • Per-battery fusing: each 5 Ah pack has its own 10 A in-line fuse before joining the bus. If one cell shorts you only lose that pack — the bus and the rest of the system keep running.
  • Ignition relay: a standard automotive 30 A SPDT closes when the ACC line goes hot, energising the 12→20 V boost and the powered USB hub (Domain B). When parked, the relay opens and Domain B is fully off — no S3 ghost draw, no USB hub leakage.
  • Standby calculation: 25 Ah ÷ 60 mA (Domain A draw) limited to 50 % DoD = ~17 days standby on a 5-battery pack before the LVD trips. 4 packs (20 Ah) gives ~14 days; 6 packs (30 Ah) gives ~21 days.

BIOS Power Settings

Two BIOS settings are critical for in-car operation. Access the M910q BIOS by pressing F1 during boot.

Setting Location Value Purpose
After Power Loss Power → After Power Loss Power On Auto-start when 12 V returns (e.g., after cranking dip or LVD reconnect)
Wake on USB Power → Wake on USB Enabled Allows the Arduino to wake the M910q from S3 suspend via USB activity
After Power Loss must be "Power On"

If this is set to "Last State" or "Power Off", the M910q will not restart automatically after a cranking voltage dip causes the boost converter to briefly lose output. "Power On" ensures the PC always boots when power is available.

S3 Suspend Configuration

Verify S3 Support

Check that the kernel supports deep (S3) sleep: 

cat /sys/power/mem_sleep

Expected output with S3 active (note the brackets indicating the current selection): 

s2idle [deep]

Enable S3 if Only s2idle is Available

If the output shows [s2idle] deep (s2idle selected by default) or just [s2idle], add the following kernel parameter to force deep sleep: 

# Edit GRUB defaults
sudo nano /etc/default/grub

# Add to GRUB_CMDLINE_LINUX_DEFAULT:
GRUB_CMDLINE_LINUX_DEFAULT="quiet mem_sleep_default=deep"

# Rebuild GRUB config
sudo update-grub
sudo reboot

logind Configuration

Disable systemd-logind's built-in power button handling so that acpid can manage it instead: 

# /etc/systemd/logind.conf
[Login]
HandlePowerKey=ignore

This prevents logind from intercepting the power button press before acpid can run the custom suspend script.

acpid Power Button Handler

The acpid daemon listens for ACPI power button events and runs bcm-power-toggle.sh to perform a clean suspend: 

# /etc/acpi/events/power-button
event=button/power
action=/opt/bcm/scripts/bcm-power-toggle.sh

Power Button Flow (bcm-power-toggle.sh)

When the power button is pressed (or the ignition watcher triggers a suspend), the bcm-power-toggle.sh script performs a clean shutdown sequence before entering S3:

  1. Stop bcm-headunit service — Gracefully shuts down the Flask backend, closes camera streams, and releases audio devices.
  2. Disable all USB wake sources — Prevents spurious wake events from USB devices moving during driving.
  3. Unbind Huawei LTE modem — The Huawei E3372 HiLink modem is known to prevent successful S3 suspend on many systems. Unbinding it from the USB driver before suspend avoids this failure.
  4. Disable XHCI and PS2K ACPI wake sources — Disables the USB controller (XHCI) and PS/2 keyboard controller wake sources to prevent unintended wakeups.
  5. systemctl suspend — Enters ACPI S3 deep sleep. Power draw drops to ~0.2 A at 12 V. 
#!/bin/bash
# /opt/bcm/scripts/bcm-power-toggle.sh

# Step 1: Stop BCM
systemctl stop bcm-headunit

# Step 2: Disable USB wake sources
for f in /sys/bus/usb/devices/*/power/wakeup; do
    echo disabled > "$f" 2>/dev/null
done

# Step 3: Unbind Huawei LTE modem (prevents suspend failure)
MODEM_ID=$(lsusb | grep -i huawei | grep -oP '\d+:\d+' | head -1)
if [ -n "$MODEM_ID" ]; then
    for dev in /sys/bus/usb/devices/*; do
        if [ -f "$dev/idVendor" ]; then
            vendor=$(cat "$dev/idVendor")
            product=$(cat "$dev/idProduct")
            if lsusb -d "${vendor}:${product}" 2>/dev/null | grep -qi huawei; then
                echo "$(basename $dev)" > /sys/bus/usb/drivers/usb/unbind 2>/dev/null
            fi
        fi
    done
fi

# Step 4: Disable XHCI and PS2K wake sources
for src in XHCI PS2K; do
    grep -q "enabled" /proc/acpi/wakeup | grep "$src" && \
        echo "$src" > /proc/acpi/wakeup
done

# Step 5: Suspend
systemctl suspend

Resume Flow (bcm-resume.service)

A systemd service runs after every S3 resume to restore USB devices and restart the BCM application: 

# /etc/systemd/system/bcm-resume.service
[Unit]
Description=BCM resume handler
After=suspend.target

[Service]
Type=oneshot
ExecStart=/opt/bcm/scripts/bcm-resume.sh

[Install]
WantedBy=suspend.target

Resume Script Steps

  1. Wait 2 seconds for hardware — Gives USB controllers and attached devices time to re-enumerate after waking from S3.
  2. Rebind USB root hubs — Re-attaches the USB root hub devices to the kernel driver. This forces re-enumeration of all USB devices, including the Huawei LTE modem that was unbound before suspend.
  3. Start bcm-headunit service — Restarts the Flask backend, camera streams, and audio pipeline. 
#!/bin/bash
# /opt/bcm/scripts/bcm-resume.sh

# Step 1: Wait for hardware to settle
sleep 2

# Step 2: Rebind USB root hubs (LTE modem reconnects)
for rhub in /sys/bus/usb/devices/usb*/; do
    devname=$(basename "$rhub")
    echo "$devname" > /sys/bus/usb/drivers/usb/unbind 2>/dev/null
    sleep 0.5
    echo "$devname" > /sys/bus/usb/drivers/usb/bind 2>/dev/null
done

# Step 3: Start BCM
systemctl start bcm-headunit

Ignition Watcher

On the x86 platform, the ignition watcher uses the Arduino input board to detect the car's ignition state. The car's 12 V ignition signal is connected to the Arduino via a PC817 optoisolator, which provides electrical isolation between the car's ignition circuit and the Arduino's digital input.

Hardware Signal Path

Ignition 12V ─[1K resistor]─ PC817 LED side
                                    │
                              PC817 transistor side ─ Arduino D2 (INPUT_PULLUP)
                                    │
                                   GND

When the ignition is ON, the PC817's internal LED illuminates, the transistor conducts, and Arduino pin D2 reads LOW. When the ignition is OFF, D2 reads HIGH (pulled up internally). The Arduino sends an IGN:ON or IGN:OFF message over USB serial to the M910q.

bcm-ignition-watcher.service

A systemd service monitors the ignition state reported by the Arduino and triggers suspend/resume accordingly: 

# /etc/systemd/system/bcm-ignition-watcher.service
[Unit]
Description=BCM ignition watcher
After=bcm-headunit.service
Requires=bcm-headunit.service

[Service]
Type=simple
ExecStart=/opt/bcm/scripts/bcm-ignition-watcher.sh
Restart=on-failure
RestartSec=5

[Install]
WantedBy=multi-user.target

Ignition State Transitions

Event Action Details
Ignition OFF Stop BCM → suspend after 5 s delay The 5-second delay prevents false triggers from momentary ignition switch bouncing during engine cranking
Ignition ON Resume → restart BCM The Arduino USB activity wakes the M910q from S3 (Wake on USB enabled in BIOS); the resume service restarts BCM

Configuration

The ignition watcher is enabled in the BCM configuration file: 

# /opt/bcm/config.yaml (relevant section)
power:
  ignition_watcher:
    autostart: true
Manual override

The physical power button on the M910q always works as a manual suspend/resume toggle, regardless of the ignition watcher state. If autostart is set to false, the ignition watcher service will not start automatically, and suspend/resume must be triggered manually via the power button.

Troubleshooting

Instant Wake After Suspend

The M910q enters S3 but immediately wakes up:

  • A USB device is triggering a wake event. Check which device caused the last wake: 
    cat /proc/acpi/wakeup
dmesg | grep -i "wake"
  • Ensure the power-toggle script disables all USB wake sources before suspending. The Huawei LTE modem is the most common culprit — verify it is unbound.
  • Check if XHCI or PS2K wake sources are still enabled in /proc/acpi/wakeup and disable them in the script.

Full 40-Second Boot Instead of S3 Resume

If the system takes ~40 seconds to reach the dashboard instead of 3–5 seconds, it is performing a full cold boot rather than an S3 resume:

  • The SLA battery buffer may have hit the LVD cutoff (11.0 V), causing a full power loss. Check bus voltage with a multimeter at the LVD input; under 11.0 V means at least one SLA pack is below ~50 % SoC and needs alternator charging.
  • The boost converter may have shut down during engine cranking. Verify the SLA buffer bus is properly wired with the Schottky diode isolating it from the car battery, and that the per-battery 10 A fuses are intact.
  • BIOS "After Power Loss" may be set to "Last State" instead of "Power On". A power loss during S3 requires auto-restart.

No S3 Available (Only s2idle)

Symptom Cause Fix
[s2idle] only in mem_sleep Kernel defaulting to s2idle (modern standby) Add mem_sleep_default=deep to GRUB_CMDLINE_LINUX_DEFAULT and run update-grub
s2idle only, no deep option listed BIOS does not advertise S3 support (some BIOS updates remove it) Check for a BIOS setting "Sleep State" or "Suspend Mode" and set it to "Linux" or "S3". Downgrade BIOS if the option was removed in a newer version.
Suspend hangs (screen goes black but fans keep running) A device driver is blocking suspend Check dmesg | grep -i "suspend" for the failing driver. The Huawei LTE modem is the most common cause — ensure it is unbound before suspend.

Ignition Watcher Not Triggering

  • Verify the Arduino is sending IGN:ON / IGN:OFF messages: 
    cat /dev/ttyACM0
  • Check that the PC817 optoisolator is wired correctly — the LED side connects to the car's ignition 12 V through a 1 KΩ resistor, and the transistor side pulls Arduino D2 low when ignition is on.
  • Ensure power.ignition_watcher.autostart is set to true in /opt/bcm/config.yaml.
  • Check the service logs: 
    sudo journalctl -u bcm-ignition-watcher.service -f
