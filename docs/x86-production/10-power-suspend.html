Power & Suspend Power supply, battery buffer, S3 suspend/resume, and ignition watcher for M910q in-car deployment

Overview

Authoritative source

This page summarises the buffered power design. All numbers come from docs/ZASILANIE_BUFOROWANE.md (ZB, Polish), which wins wherever this page differs. Terminal-by-terminal wiring: docs/SCHEMATY_POLACZEN.md.

The Lenovo M910q uses a 65 W, 20 V supply on a Lenovo slim-tip plug and runs only in the 19–21 V window. In the car a potted 12 V → 20 V, 10 A (200 W) step-up converter in the trunk feeds it; at the M910q's full 65 W it works at about a third of its nameplate rating, so no CPU power limit (RAPL) is needed — bcm-power-cap stays as an option for quieter, cooler running.

When the engine cranks, the car's 12 V rail sags to 6–8 V for 200–600 ms. A buffer bank of seven CSB HR1221W AGM batteries (12 V / 5.1 Ah each, in parallel: 35.7 Ah) keeps the bus above 12 V through it. The bank sits in two steel baskets (4 + 3) in the trunk and is charged from the car by two XY-SK120 buck-boost CC-CV modules (CV 14.40 V, CC 5.0 A each) through the charge relay K1, which closes only with ignition / D+. Each charger has its own XH-M603 overvoltage disconnect (15.30 V). An XH-M609 LVD (11.00 V off / 12.60 V on) protects the bank from deep discharge.

Everything hangs on one buffered bus behind the LVD — the M910q, USB hub, both displays, the three Arduino boards, HM-10, RXB6 and the relay module. There is no ignition relay in front of the loads: ignition is only a signal. Nano #2 (sensor hub) reads it through a PC817 and pulses the M910q power button through a relay. With the engine off K1 is open, so the starter battery never feeds the head unit while parked and the car always starts, however empty the bank is.

Three machine states

State How it is reached Draw from the bank Time to 50 % DoD (35.7 Ah)
Running Ignition ON → short pulse → wake from S3 ≈ 50–75 W, i.e. ≈ 4–6 A (5 A assumed) ~3.6 h with the engine off
S3 Ignition OFF → short pulse → suspend 200–460 mA + idle current of the step-up 1.6–3.7 days (without the step-up idle current)
Off 2 h after ignition OFF → long 5 s pulse 50–150 mA + idle current of the step-up 5.0–14.9 days (without the step-up idle current)
Standby figures are an upper bound

The S3 and Off draws were measured or estimated with the earlier, weaker step-up module. The new 20 V converter hangs on the bus all the time and adds its own idle current, which sellers do not publish — measure it (ZB §3.4, §11). Until then the times above are the longest you can expect, not a promise. The M910q cuts USB power in S3, so the displays go dark with it, Wake on USB is useless, and the Arduino boards must have their own 5 V from the MP1584 buck — otherwise nothing could press the power button.

Bank monitoring (optional)

With FEATURE_BATT_ADC or FEATURE_BATT_INA226 built into the sensor hub firmware and modules.battery: true, src/power/battery.py reports the bank state with AGM thresholds: full 12.85 V, nominal 12.40 V (reference only), low 11.80 V, critical 11.20 V — just above the 11.0 V LVD so BCM can warn first. It never switches anything off; the hardware LVD does (ZB §13.4).

Key Design Points

  • Two chargers, not one: while driving the bus draws ~5 A. One XY-SK120 at 5 A would leave about −1…+1 A net (≈ 0) for the bank at a 4–6 A load, so it would not charge while driving; two give ~10 A, i.e. ~5 A net. Buck-boost modules keep regulating whether the alternator gives 12.5 or 14.7 V, so no isolating diode is needed. There is no galvanic isolation — hence the single ground point (ZB §5.6). An isolated B2B charger remains a more expensive alternative (ZB §5.7).
  • Overvoltage disconnect: one XH-M603 per charger opens above 15.30 V if a charger fails high. The board carries ~8–10 A in practice, so one module cannot take both.
  • LVD (XH-M609): cuts the whole bus at 11.00 V and reconnects at 12.60 V, i.e. only after real charging. It must switch the positive line — check with an ohmmeter that VIN− and VOUT− are always connected.
  • Fuses at the source: F1 20 A within 30 cm of the starter battery, FB1…FB7 10 A on every pack, F7 20 A and F12 15 A at the bank block in MIDI/AMI or ANL class (the bank can deliver ~2.2 kA into a short), branch fuses F8–F13 in the trunk at the start of each wire running forward.
  • Converter in the trunk: 20 V over the ~4 m run to the M910q loses less than 12 V would (2 × 4 mm²: ~0.1 V at 65 W), and the LVD measures the bank itself rather than the end of a long wire.
  • Amplifier on its own branch: straight from the starter battery with its own fuse and local ground. Its peaks would exceed the 20 A LVD relay; it shares only the REM signal and the RCA screen with the head unit.

BIOS Power Settings

Two BIOS settings are critical for in-car operation. Access the M910q BIOS by pressing F1 during boot.

Setting Location Value Purpose
After Power Loss Power → After Power Loss Power On Auto-start when 12 V returns (e.g., after cranking dip or LVD reconnect)
Wake on USB Power → Wake on USB Disabled The M910q cuts USB power in S3, so nothing on USB (Arduino, car phone) can wake it — it only causes false wake-ups. Wake goes through the power button: the relay driven by Nano #2 (sensor hub) in the buffered build, or a manual press
After Power Loss must be "Power On"

If this is set to "Last State" or "Power Off", the M910q will not restart automatically after a cranking voltage dip causes the boost converter to briefly lose output. "Power On" ensures the PC always boots when power is available.

S3 Suspend Configuration

Verify S3 Support

Check that the kernel supports deep (S3) sleep: 

cat /sys/power/mem_sleep

Expected output with S3 active (note the brackets indicating the current selection): 

s2idle [deep]

Enable S3 if Only s2idle is Available

If the output shows [s2idle] deep (s2idle selected by default) or just [s2idle], add the following kernel parameter to force deep sleep: 

# Edit GRUB defaults
sudo nano /etc/default/grub

# Add to GRUB_CMDLINE_LINUX_DEFAULT:
GRUB_CMDLINE_LINUX_DEFAULT="quiet mem_sleep_default=deep"

# Rebuild GRUB config
sudo update-grub
sudo reboot

logind Configuration

Disable systemd-logind's built-in power button handling so that acpid can manage it instead: 

# /etc/systemd/logind.conf
[Login]
HandlePowerKey=ignore

This prevents logind from intercepting the power button press before acpid can run the custom suspend script.

acpid Power Button Handler

The acpid daemon listens for ACPI power button events and runs bcm-power-toggle.sh to perform a clean suspend: 

# /etc/acpi/events/power-button
event=button/power
action=/opt/bcm/scripts/bcm-power-toggle.sh

Power Button Flow (bcm-power-toggle.sh)

When the power button is pressed (or the ignition watcher triggers a suspend), the bcm-power-toggle.sh script performs a clean shutdown sequence before entering S3:

  1. Stop bcm-headunit service — Gracefully shuts down the Flask backend, closes camera streams, and releases audio devices.
  2. Disable all USB wake sources — Prevents spurious wake events from USB devices moving during driving.
  3. Unbind Huawei LTE modem — The Huawei E3372 HiLink modem is known to prevent successful S3 suspend on many systems. Unbinding it from the USB driver before suspend avoids this failure.
  4. Disable XHCI and PS2K ACPI wake sources — Disables the USB controller (XHCI) and PS/2 keyboard controller wake sources to prevent unintended wakeups.
  5. systemctl suspend — Enters ACPI S3 deep sleep. The whole head unit then draws ~200–460 mA from the bank, plus the step-up idle current (see Overview). 
#!/bin/bash
# /opt/bcm/scripts/bcm-power-toggle.sh

# Step 1: Stop BCM
systemctl stop bcm-headunit

# Step 2: Disable USB wake sources
for f in /sys/bus/usb/devices/*/power/wakeup; do
    echo disabled > "$f" 2>/dev/null
done

# Step 3: Unbind Huawei LTE modem (prevents suspend failure)
MODEM_ID=$(lsusb | grep -i huawei | grep -oP '\d+:\d+' | head -1)
if [ -n "$MODEM_ID" ]; then
    for dev in /sys/bus/usb/devices/*; do
        if [ -f "$dev/idVendor" ]; then
            vendor=$(cat "$dev/idVendor")
            product=$(cat "$dev/idProduct")
            if lsusb -d "${vendor}:${product}" 2>/dev/null | grep -qi huawei; then
                echo "$(basename $dev)" > /sys/bus/usb/drivers/usb/unbind 2>/dev/null
            fi
        fi
    done
fi

# Step 4: Disable XHCI and PS2K wake sources
for src in XHCI PS2K; do
    grep -q "enabled" /proc/acpi/wakeup | grep "$src" && \
        echo "$src" > /proc/acpi/wakeup
done

# Step 5: Suspend
systemctl suspend

Resume Flow (bcm-resume.service)

A systemd service runs after every S3 resume to restore USB devices and restart the BCM application: 

# /etc/systemd/system/bcm-resume.service
[Unit]
Description=BCM resume handler
After=suspend.target

[Service]
Type=oneshot
ExecStart=/opt/bcm/scripts/bcm-resume.sh

[Install]
WantedBy=suspend.target

Resume Script Steps

  1. Wait 2 seconds for hardware — Gives USB controllers and attached devices time to re-enumerate after waking from S3.
  2. Rebind USB root hubs — Re-attaches the USB root hub devices to the kernel driver. This forces re-enumeration of all USB devices, including the Huawei LTE modem that was unbound before suspend.
  3. Start bcm-headunit service — Restarts the Flask backend, camera streams, and audio pipeline. 
#!/bin/bash
# /opt/bcm/scripts/bcm-resume.sh

# Step 1: Wait for hardware to settle
sleep 2

# Step 2: Rebind USB root hubs (LTE modem reconnects)
for rhub in /sys/bus/usb/devices/usb*/; do
    devname=$(basename "$rhub")
    echo "$devname" > /sys/bus/usb/drivers/usb/unbind 2>/dev/null
    sleep 0.5
    echo "$devname" > /sys/bus/usb/drivers/usb/bind 2>/dev/null
done

# Step 3: Start BCM
systemctl start bcm-headunit

Ignition Watcher

On the x86 platform, the ignition watcher uses Arduino Nano #2 (sensor hub, arduino/sensor_hub) to detect the car's ignition state. The car's 12 V ignition signal is connected to the Nano via a PC817 optoisolator, which provides electrical isolation between the car's ignition circuit and the Arduino's digital input. The same board drives the relay whose contacts sit in parallel with the M910q power button (FEATURE_PWRBTN, pin A0) and reads the front-panel power LED (FEATURE_PWRLED, pin A1). It is powered from the MP1584 5 V buck, so it keeps working while the M910q sleeps and its USB ports are dead.

Hardware Signal Path

Ignition 12V ─[1K resistor]─ PC817 LED side
                                    │
                              PC817 transistor side ─ Nano #2 D9 (INPUT_PULLUP)
                                    │
                                   GND

When the ignition is ON, the PC817's internal LED illuminates, the transistor conducts, and pin D9 reads LOW. When the ignition is OFF, D9 reads HIGH (pulled up internally). The sensor hub reports IGN:1 or IGN:0 over USB serial (115200 baud) while the M910q is awake, and independently pulses the power button: short pulse on ignition OFF (S3) and ON (wake), a long 5 s pulse after 2 h with the ignition off (full power-off).

bcm-ignition-watcher.service

A systemd service monitors the ignition state reported by the Arduino and triggers suspend/resume accordingly: 

# /etc/systemd/system/bcm-ignition-watcher.service
[Unit]
Description=BCM ignition watcher
After=bcm-headunit.service
Requires=bcm-headunit.service

[Service]
Type=simple
ExecStart=/opt/bcm/scripts/bcm-ignition-watcher.sh
Restart=on-failure
RestartSec=5

[Install]
WantedBy=multi-user.target

Ignition State Transitions

Event Action Details
Ignition OFF Stop BCM → suspend after 5 s delay The 5-second delay prevents false triggers from momentary ignition switch bouncing during engine cranking
Ignition ON Resume → restart BCM The Nano #2 relay pulses the M910q power button, which wakes it from S3 (USB is unpowered in S3, so Wake on USB stays disabled); the system-sleep hook (bcm-sleep) restores BCM after resume

Configuration

The ignition watcher is enabled in the BCM configuration file: 

# /opt/bcm/config.yaml (relevant section)
power:
  ignition_watcher:
    autostart: true
Manual override

The physical power button on the M910q always works as a manual suspend/resume toggle, regardless of the ignition watcher state. If autostart is set to false, the ignition watcher service will not start automatically, and suspend/resume must be triggered manually via the power button.

Boot Timeline

Cold boot and S3 resume timings for the M910q with NVMe SSD and optimized BIOS settings (fast boot enabled, boot device list minimized):

Time Event
0–2 s Lenovo BIOS POST (fast boot enabled, no splash logo)
2–4 s Kernel loads (silent boot, GRUB menu hidden with 0 s timeout)
4–5 s DRM device ready → splash video starts playing on both displays
5–10 s Flask backend starts behind splash (loading config, initializing serial, cameras)
10–12 s Splash ends → X11 starts → Chromium launches in kiosk mode
~12 s Dashboard visible — full BCM interface on both screens
Mode Time to Dashboard
Cold boot (power on) ~12 s
S3 resume (suspend → wake) ~3–5 s from power button to dashboard
S3 resume is 3× faster

With S3 suspend, the driver sees the BCM dashboard in 3–5 seconds after turning the ignition key. This is fast enough that the interface is ready before the driver finishes adjusting their mirrors. This is the primary reason for using S3 suspend instead of a full shutdown.

Troubleshooting

Instant Wake After Suspend

The M910q enters S3 but immediately wakes up:

  • A USB device is triggering a wake event. Check which device caused the last wake: 
    cat /proc/acpi/wakeup
dmesg | grep -i "wake"
  • Ensure the power-toggle script disables all USB wake sources before suspending. The Huawei LTE modem is the most common culprit — verify it is unbound.
  • Check if XHCI or PS2K wake sources are still enabled in /proc/acpi/wakeup and disable them in the script.

Full 40-Second Boot Instead of S3 Resume

If the system takes ~40 seconds to reach the dashboard instead of 3–5 seconds, it is performing a full cold boot rather than an S3 resume:

  • The AGM bank may have hit the LVD cutoff (11.00 V; the bus returns only at 12.60 V), causing a full power loss. Check the bank voltage with a multimeter at the LVD input (VIN); near 11 V the bank is deeply discharged and needs a long drive or a mains AGM charger.
  • The 20 V step-up may have dropped out. Check that the bank holds the bus above 12 V while cranking (if it sags, look for blown FB1…FB7 pack fuses or a loose basket link), that F8 and the 5 A output fuse are intact, and that the converter still regulates at 11 V input (ZB §3.4). Also check the long-pulse rule: after 2 h with the ignition off the sensor hub powers the M910q off on purpose, so the next start is a cold boot.
  • BIOS "After Power Loss" may be set to "Last State" instead of "Power On". A power loss during S3 requires auto-restart.

No S3 Available (Only s2idle)

Symptom Cause Fix
[s2idle] only in mem_sleep Kernel defaulting to s2idle (modern standby) Add mem_sleep_default=deep to GRUB_CMDLINE_LINUX_DEFAULT and run update-grub
s2idle only, no deep option listed BIOS does not advertise S3 support (some BIOS updates remove it) Check for a BIOS setting "Sleep State" or "Suspend Mode" and set it to "Linux" or "S3". Downgrade BIOS if the option was removed in a newer version.
Suspend hangs (screen goes black but fans keep running) A device driver is blocking suspend Check dmesg | grep -i "suspend" for the failing driver. The Huawei LTE modem is the most common cause — ensure it is unbound before suspend.

Ignition Watcher Not Triggering

  • Verify the Arduino is sending IGN:ON / IGN:OFF messages: 
    cat /dev/ttyACM0
  • Check that the PC817 optoisolator is wired correctly — the LED side connects to the car's ignition 12 V through a 1 KΩ resistor, and the transistor side pulls Arduino D2 low when ignition is on.
  • Ensure power.ignition_watcher.autostart is set to true in /opt/bcm/config.yaml.
  • Check the service logs: 
    sudo journalctl -u bcm-ignition-watcher.service -f
