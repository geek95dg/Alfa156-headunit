BCM v8.5 — Lenovo M910q Production Build Complete guide for deploying BCM headunit in Alfa Romeo 156

Project Overview

BCM (Body Computer Module) is a custom head unit that replaces the original Alfa Romeo 156 stereo and instrument cluster with a modern, Linux-based infotainment and vehicle management system. It provides a dual-screen dashboard (10.1" 1280×800 touchscreen + optional 6.86" 1280×480 second display), full body-computer functions (fuel, temperatures, doors, OBD-II diagnostics), a 4.1 audio system with 10-band EQ, quad-camera DVR with auto-switching, Android Auto, GPS tracking, and an always-on Arduino subsystem (4-window remote keyfob + BLE-gated trunk button). Everything runs from a buffer bank of seven CSB HR1221W AGM batteries (35.7 Ah) in two baskets in the trunk, so the always-on parts keep working with the BCM asleep in S3. The original Alfa 156 central lock and alarm are left untouched.

The Lenovo ThinkCentre M910q Tiny is the recommended x86 production platform: a full x86_64 mini-PC that fits inside the car dashboard (1-litre form factor). The M910q offers dramatically more processing power, native VAAPI hardware decode, NVMe storage, dual DisplayPort outputs, and runs standard Debian without ARM kernel patching. Used units are widely available at competitive prices, making this the most practical production build.

Why x86?

An x86 mini-PC like the M910q removes every ARM-specific pain point: no custom kernel builds, no display driver patches, no WiFi firmware issues. Debian runs unmodified, VAAPI hardware decode works out of the box, and suspend/resume (S3) is rock-solid. The trade-off is a higher power draw (~15 W idle, up to 65 W at 20 V), handled by a 200 W 12 V → 20 V step-up converter and the AGM buffer bank described in 10 — Power & Suspend.

Build Phases Overview

The build is divided into five stages. Each stage is independently functional — you can drive with Stage 1 alone and add the rest over time. Prices are in PLN (Q1 2026 estimates, Polish retailers). The M910q is typically 200–400 PLN used; most of the Stage 1 cost is the buffered power system and the displays.

Stage 1 Core System

M910q + displays + audio (DAC + ready-made amplifier) + K-Line OBD-II + buffered power (AGM bank, 2× XY-SK120, XH-M603, XH-M609, 20 V step-up)

~1 830 – 3 620 PLN

Stage 2 Cameras + Sensors

4-way AHD camera set + USB grabber + parking sensors + DS18B20 + dashcam SSD

~450 – 800 PLN

Stage 3 Input + Connectivity

~260 – 420 PLN

~115 – 195 PLN

Why the M910q won

Standard Debian with no kernel patching. Native ACPI suspend/resume (3-second wake). VAAPI hardware decode with no driver issues. 256 GB NVMe eliminates microSD reliability concerns. Six USB 3.0 ports remove the need for a hub in most configurations. Used units at 200–400 PLN make it price-competitive with the OPi 5 Pro at 350–450 PLN new.
